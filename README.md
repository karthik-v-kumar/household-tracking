# Stocked

Shared household grocery lists, pantry inventory, and replacement filters.

Built for a two-person household that was using iPhone Reminders — and tired of
re-adding the same weekend items, splitting lists by store, forgetting when bulk
goods like toilet paper were running out, and losing track of furnace and Tesla
cabin filters.

## What it does

- **Lists per store.** Grocery, warehouse, and pharmacy to start — add Costco, the farmers market, Target, or anything else, and delete the ones you do not use. Each list has its own name, icon, and color.
- **Usuals tray.** Star items you buy often. They sit in a tray on that list so you can tap (or drag onto the list) only what you need this week — you do not have to take every usual every time. “Add remaining” is there if you do want the rest.
- **Clear bought.** Checking off an item does not delete the memory of it. After the trip, clear bought items; usuals stay in the catalog. Buying a matching filter spare also restocks the Filters shelf.
- **Inventory (Pantry).** Track bulk / slow-burn goods with Full / Okay / Low / Out. Set a typical lifespan so the app can warn you before you run out. One tap adds low items to the right list. Buying them restocks inventory.
- **Filters.** Track consumables that change on a schedule — furnace air filter, air purifier, fridge water, vacuum, air fryer, Tesla Model Y cabin filters (qty 2), or anything you add. Each item has a replace-every interval, last-replaced date, and spare count. If you are due and have no spare on the shelf, it goes on a shopping list. Mark **Replaced** after you swap it in; the spare count drops and the clock resets.
- **Household sharing.** Create a household, share a short invite code. Both people stay signed in on their phones and see the same data.

## Accounts

Sign in with Google, X, or email and password. Sessions persist on the device after the first login.

## Data

Household lists, catalog (usuals), pantry inventory, and filter replacements live in Postgres.

## Design

Cream ivory and charcoal, Instrument Serif + Instrument Sans, pill wordmark,
hairline panels, and rounded-full buttons — civic-luxury language in the spirit
of National Design Studio public sites. No Nintendo chrome, no government
wordmarks.
